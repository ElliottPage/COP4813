An Invader of Florida's Reefs

Lionfish are native to the Indo-Pacific, but they have become invasive in the Atlantic, including waters around Florida. Their striking stripes and flowing fins hide a serious problem for native marine life.

Lionfish eat small native fish and crustaceans, including young fish that help support reef communities. They also compete with native predators, such as snapper and grouper for food.

With few natural predators in their invaded range, lionfish can disrupt the balance of reef ecosystems and reduce the variety of native marine life.

Real lionfish have venomous spines. Never touch or attempt to handle one.

Information: NOAA National Ocean Service
